# Q&A Prep

## Why not just use follower count?

Followers measure reach, but not engagement, attention growth, performance credibility, or sponsor fit. A player with fewer followers but stronger engagement and fast momentum can be a better opportunity.

## Why these weights?

The weights are simple on purpose. Performance, reach, and attention receive the most weight because they are the clearest public signals. Engagement, momentum, and brand fit add context. In the final version, we should run sensitivity checks to show whether the recommendations are stable when weights change.

## What does marketability gap mean?

It compares actual marketability to the marketability we would expect from basketball performance. A negative gap suggests a player may be under-marketed. A positive gap suggests the player has brand value above what performance alone predicts.

## How would the Mavericks use this?

They could use it to decide which players deserve more content support, which players fit certain sponsor categories, and which rising players may have commercial upside before the market fully prices it in.

## What data would improve the model?

Internal ticketing data, merchandise sales by player, local fan survey data, sponsor conversion metrics, audience demographics, and official social impression data.

## Biggest limitation?

Public data is incomplete. Social metrics can be noisy, and manual brand scores need a clear rubric. The model should support decisions, not make them by itself.

## Why is the dashboard useful?

It makes the framework reusable. Judges can see that the method is not only a one-time slide deck; it can be updated as new data comes in.
